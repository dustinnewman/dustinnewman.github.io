Posterior Visualizer

September 4, 2026

Bayes' rule is four symbols and a horizontal line, and I have never once found that enough to see what it does. So here is the whole thing taken apart: a coin with unknown bias θ\theta, a handful of flips, and every term of the rule laid out in the order you would actually compute it.

Play with it. Drag θ\theta and watch the likelihood row rewrite itself. Flip a coin from heads to tails and watch the posterior slide left. Widen the prior and watch the data take over.

Three things are worth noticing:

  • The likelihood is not a distribution over θ\theta. It is a number you get by picking a θ\theta and asking how well it explains the flips you have. That is why it is computed here rather than set.
  • The evidence p(X)p(X) is one number. It does not depend on θ\theta, so dividing by it can only rescale the curve — it can never move the peak. All of the shape of the posterior comes from prior ×\times likelihood.
  • With a Beta prior the posterior is Beta again, and the update is just counting: α\alpha gains a head, β\beta gains a tail. Conjugacy is the reason the picture stays this clean.

The prior here is Beta(α,β)\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha, \beta) because it is the natural distribution over a probability. It lives on [0,1][0, 1], it is flat when α=β=1\alpha = \beta = 1, and it can be pushed toward either end or squeezed toward the middle with two knobs. A Gaussian cannot do that without leaking probability past the ends of the interval.

The likelihood assumes the flips are independent given θ\theta, so it is the product θh(1−θ)t\theta^h (1-\theta)^t over the hh heads and tt tails. Note that XX is the sequence of flips, not the count, so there is no binomial coefficient out front. It would cancel in the ratio anyway.

The evidence is the integral of that product against the prior, and for a Beta prior it has a closed form:

p(X)=∫01θh(1−θ)t p(θ) dθ=B(α+h, β+t)B(α, β)p(X) = \int_0^1 \theta^{h}(1-\theta)^{t}\, p(\theta)\, \mathrm{d}\theta = \frac{B(\alpha + h,\, \beta + t)}{B(\alpha,\, \beta)}

so nothing on this page is a numerical approximation. Put those together and the posterior is Beta(α+h, β+t)\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha + h,\, \beta + t) — the same curve, redrawn with the counts folded in.
